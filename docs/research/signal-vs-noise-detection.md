# Research: telling signal from noise, and what it costs

Period: 2026-08-23.
Related decisions: [ADR-0010](../adr/0010-classifier-vox-gate.md),
[ADR-0008](../adr/0008-rf-squelch-gate.md),
[ADR-0013](../adr/0013-windowed-sinc-channel-filter.md).

## Why this exists

`VoxRecorder` gates recording on `StreamClassifier`, which scores demodulated
audio on envelope modulation, spectral flatness and speech-band share, then
compares that score against 0.45 with a 15 dB loudness override
([ADR-0010](../adr/0010-classifier-vox-gate.md)). Those constants were chosen by
feel. The obvious question is why they have to be: why can the classifier not
just *decide*, deterministically, whether a block is static.

The short answer is that it does decide deterministically — the same block
always scores the same. What it cannot be is *infallible*, and that limit is
informational, not computational. No amount of extra CPU removes it. This
document records why, what the real limits are, and which of them radiotui is
running into unnecessarily.

## The categories genuinely overlap

A well-designed signal is engineered to look like noise. Shannon's coding
results make maximum entropy the goal of good encoding, and maximum entropy is
the definition of white noise. This is not a curiosity — it is the design
target. Spread-spectrum transmissions are built to be
[indistinguishable from ordinary line static](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/5081645),
appearing only as low-level random noise to anyone without the key. Encrypted
traffic is noise-shaped by construction; if it were not, that would be a
cryptographic defect.

So "is this noise?" has no general answer from the waveform alone. Every
statistic the classifier measures is a proxy for *does this look structured*,
and structure is exactly what good encoding removes.

## The formal frame

Detection is a hypothesis test — H₀: noise only, H₁: signal plus noise. The
Neyman–Pearson lemma gives the optimal test, but optimal means *the best
available tradeoff*, not *error-free*: you move along a ROC curve trading missed
detections against false alarms. Under additive Gaussian noise the two
distributions always overlap, so no operating point has both zero false alarms
and zero misses. The 0.45 threshold is a point on that curve. Picking it is a
policy decision about which error hurts more, not a bug to be fixed.

## The hard limit: SNR walls

[Tandra and Sahai (2008)](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~sahai/Papers/tandra_sahai_revised_submission.jstsp.final.pdf)
prove that noise uncertainty imposes a floor no observation time can beat. For
an energy detector (radiometer) with noise uncertainty factor ρ:

```
SNR_wall = (ρ² − 1) / ρ
```

Their intuition for it is the clearest part: the radiometer fails once signal
power drops below *the uncertainty in the noise power*, because at that point
"the presence of the signal is indistinguishable from a slightly larger value
for the noise". Below the wall, more samples do not help — the required sample
count diverges.

The stronger result is the one that matters for any plan to fix this with
better algorithms:

> Given knowledge of the signal, we prove that both coherent and cyclostationary
> feature detectors are also non-robust to model uncertainties when channel
> coherence times are finite. […] These results strongly suggest that all
> detectors are non-robust.

Knowing what you are looking for does not abolish the wall; it changes which
uncertainty binds. Blind detection is limited by how well you know the noise
power. Coherent detection is limited by **how long the channel holds still** —
the coherence time `Nc`. Integrate past it and the gain does not merely stop:
averaging a feature across independent coherent blocks drives the test statistic
toward zero, because the phase of each block is effectively uniform on the unit
circle.

The same paper offers a cheap practical lever: **noise calibration**. Reducing
uncertainty from 10 dB to 1 dB moves the wall substantially, and costs nothing
but measuring the floor when nothing is transmitting.

## What knowing the signal actually buys

Correlating against a known sequence makes the signal add coherently (∝ N) while
noise adds incoherently (∝ √N), so the gain is the time-bandwidth product.

GPS is the reference case, and it is public. From
[IS-GPS-200N](https://navcen.uscg.gov/sites/default/files/pdf/gps/IS-GPS-200N.pdf)
§3.2.1.3, verbatim:

> The PRN C/A-code for SV ID number i is a Gold code, Gi(t), of 1 millisecond in
> length at a chipping rate of 1023 kbps. The Gi(t) sequence is a linear pattern
> generated by the modulo-2 addition of two sub-sequences, G1 and G2i, each of
> which is a 1023 chip long linear pattern.

Anyone can read that, generate the codes, and correlate. Doubling the coherent
integration period buys 3 dB, because the correlated signal gains power while
uncorrelated noise does not — that is the whole mechanism, and it needs no
permission and no secret.

**A correction worth recording, because the folklore is wrong.** It is commonly
said that these techniques recover signals "below the noise floor". They do not.
FT8 decodes at −21 dB and WSPR at −28 dB *in a 2500 Hz reference bandwidth*
([PA3FWM](http://www.pa3fwm.nl/technotes/tn09b.html),
[K0NR](https://www.k0nr.com/wordpress/2025/03/weak-signal-performance/)), and
[VU2NSB](https://vu2nsb.com/digital-modes-ft8-below-noise/) is explicit that the
below-the-noise-floor claim is a myth — the negative figure is relative to a
reference bandwidth, not to noise in the signal's own occupied bandwidth. WSPR
occupies a few Hz; in its own bandwidth the SNR is positive.

The accurate statement: **a known code does not conjure signal out of noise, it
lets you discard every part of the noise that is not shaped like your signal.**
"Clearer" means "narrower effective noise bandwidth".

## The ladder of detectors

| Detector | Needs | Buys | Limited by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Energy / radiometer | nothing | least | noise-power uncertainty (the wall above) |
| Cyclostationary | symbol rate / periodicity | ~10 dB over energy detection | channel coherence time |
| Coherent / matched filter | the waveform itself | most (time-bandwidth product) | coherence time, but scales better than cyclostationary |
| Physical (noise squelch, CTCSS) | mode-specific structure | near-binary in practice | only meaningful for that mode |

Cyclostationary detection deserves a note because it is the principled middle
ground: noise is wide-sense stationary and has no cyclic correlation at any
α ≠ 0, whereas modulated signals carry hidden periodicities from their symbol
rate and carrier. The noise feature therefore vanishes as N grows while the
signal feature persists, which is why it is
[robust to noise-power uncertainty where energy detection is not](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1311.6449)
and buys roughly
[10 dB](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1708.05201) over energy detection.

**Noise squelch** is the one that matters most here, and it is what hardware
radios actually do. A squelch circuit high-passes the discriminator output above
the voice band — [typically above 6 kHz](https://www.rfwireless-world.com/terminology/understanding-squelch-circuits)
— and measures the hiss there. An FM discriminator has a rising noise spectrum,
so with no carrier there is a great deal of ultrasonic hash; when a carrier
captures the limiter, that hash collapses. The receiver "goes into limiting and
the noise is preempted by the signal". It is not a statistical guess about
content; it measures a physical consequence of carrier capture, which is why it
behaves deterministically in practice. [KA7OEI](http://ka7oei.blogspot.com/2015/11/fm-squelch-and-subaudible-tone.html?m=1)
covers the implementation alongside sub-audible tone detection.

## Availability, honestly, for the bands radiotui scans

- **Analog voice** — FM broadcast, airband AM, marine, PMR446, ham FM — is most
  of what radiotui sweeps, and there is **no spreading code to obtain**. There
  is, however, known deterministic structure: the 19 kHz FM stereo pilot, CTCSS
  (38 standard tones, 67.0–254.1 Hz) and DCS, and the AM carrier sitting at a
  known channel centre. Broadcast FM's pilot is the transmitter deliberately
  spending power to be easy to detect; Tandra and Sahai frame this as a
  capacity/robustness tradeoff — "any power spent on the pilot improves
  robustness but reduces the power available for data".
- **Digital modes** are where published structure is directly usable. POCSAG
  transmits a 576-bit alternating preamble followed by a unique 32-bit frame
  sync word ([Raveon AN142](https://www.raveon.com/pdfiles/AN142%28POCSAG%29.pdf));
  AIS, ADS-B, DMR, P25 and D-STAR all have public frame structures to correlate
  against.
- **Weak-signal amateur modes** (FT8, WSPR, JT65) are fully open protocols with
  open-source decoders — the purest case of "the codes are already available".

## What this means for radiotui

The current gate does the hardest available version of the problem: blind, with
no modulation prior, operating on **demodulated audio** — the point in the chain
where the most information has already been destroyed. That is why it needs
hand-tuned constants. It is running into a wall it did not have to approach.

The information it is not using is already in hand:

- the raw IQ, before demodulation throws away phase;
- RSSI, which [ADR-0008](../adr/0008-rf-squelch-gate.md) already exposes as an
  optional RF gate;
- for NFM, a discriminator whose high-frequency noise collapses on carrier
  capture — the quieting effect above, available for the cost of a high-pass
  filter and an RMS;
- for WFM, a 19 kHz pilot that is always present on a real broadcast station;
- for AM, a carrier at a known frequency.

None of these defeat the theory. They change the question so that priors do the
work, which is the only thing that ever has. The conclusion for this codebase is
not "write a better classifier" but **"stop asking a blind question when a
mode-specific one is available"**.

Tracked as follow-up work rather than assumed: per-mode deterministic detectors,
noise-floor calibration, and NFM channel-rate selectivity (see
[ADR-0013](../adr/0013-windowed-sinc-channel-filter.md) Consequences).
